## A hardware store closes.

On the balance sheet that's one business, four jobs, an empty storefront. Here's what actually happened: the lumber supplier two blocks over just lost a steady buyer. Three contractors now drive twenty minutes each way, costing them an hour a day they used to bill. The lunch counter has lost the weekday trade of those crews. None of those businesses were in trouble last month.

## The challenge

Relief money is genuinely scarce — a city office might have fifty thousand dollars and forty applicants. The obvious move is to rank applicants by how much trouble they're in. That produces a defensible-looking list, and it is blind to the only thing that makes a local economy different from forty separate businesses: they hold each other up.

## Your mission

Build a case, not a score. An agent a grant officer can hand a situation to and get back something they could take into a meeting and defend.

## Pick your track

- **Main Street relief** — a queue of applicants, not enough money, every case real.
- **Micro-producer continuity** — the damage is upstream and nobody's filed anything yet.
- **Anchor closure** — a big employer leaves in 90 days. There are no applicants. Find who's exposed before they know it.

## Required differentiator

BigQuery property graphs. A ranked list of at-risk merchants is a query. A cascade is a graph.

## You in hand

A Grant Memo — which business and how much, what it protects (the cascade, named), the traversal shown rather than described, why this one and not the runner-up, and what you're assuming, because a grant changes a probability, not an outcome.
